Plate III · Frontispiece

Scenario analysis framework

No one can say which climate future will arrive. A firm can, however, be ready for several. This framework sets out the method, assumptions, and governance that keeps it credible.

What changed

Resilience is now tested rather than assumed, across three climate futures, with the results deliberated at board level.

Lens · Antifragile

What was done

  • Expanded NGFS scenarios spanning orderly, disorderly and hot house world pathways, localising each to the Malaysian context and its policies, and translating them into the physical and transition risks the business would face.
  • Established a governance cycle in which results are deliberated by management and the board risk committee, and scenarios refreshed with each new NGFS release.
  • Guided the business units involved and convened them to assess the resilience of the business and its strategy under each scenario. The questions were whether investments would hold their portfolio value, whether capital would remain above statutory levels, and whether operations could continue.

Lessons learnt

A scenario is not a prediction. One prepares most carefully for the future one hopes never to meet.

Plate I

Sustainability governance

Sustainability was once filed under corporate social responsibility, as beach clean-ups and recycling drives photographed for the annual report. Yet a business that is not sustainable will not, in time, remain a business. And so the work moved from the beach to the boardroom, to who decides what and who answers for it.

What changed

Sustainability is now in the board charter, a standing item on the board’s agenda, and woven into the work of underwriting, finance and investment.

Lens · Systems thinking

What was done

  • Helped establish a board-level sustainability committee and authored its terms of reference.
  • Extended the remit of Shariah oversight to include sustainability, with sustainability matters reported to the Shariah committee as required.
  • Embedded sustainability and climate risk in the business plan and, through it, in the KPIs against which senior management is measured.

Lessons learnt

Sustainability is not settled on the top floor. It begins when executives come down, leave the air conditioning behind, and feel the heat of the world they are planning for.

Plate II

AI-assisted workflows

Reporting teams stay lean while the requirements placed upon them grow heavier, cycle upon cycle. Each round teaches a team what it takes to meet the deadline, and what must change before the next. The answer was not more hands, but a different kind of help.

What changed

The existing review is now augmented rather than replaced, and the time it returns goes to human judgement.

Lens · Iteration loops

What was done

  • Rebuilt the reporting workflow once the process had matured, bringing in AI agents to help develop the reports.
  • Tested each report against a defined body of greenwashing principles, so that every claim must show its substance.
  • Added a review of compliance with IFRS S2 requirements, flagging inaccuracies, inconsistencies and lapses in coherence across the report.

Lessons learnt

No report is ever finished, only released. Every loop leaves something to improve, and the next one puts it right.

Plate IV

Sustainability data controls

A figure in a sustainability report rarely announces that it is wrong. So the question was turned on its head. Rather than asking whether a number was right, one asked how it might be made wrong, and each answer became a control.

What changed

The figures withstood the scrutiny of auditors, as they should when the second line of defence has already done its work.

Lens · Invert, invert, invert

What was done

  • Traced each reported figure back through its source, its method and its point of entry, and asked at every stage how it could fail.
  • Converted each point of failure into a control, so that every figure is checked at the stage where it is most likely to go wrong.
  • Established a sustainability reporting guide that sets out the maker-checker process, validation controls, path of review and approval, and where accountability rests at each step.

Lessons learnt

The error is rarely where one expects it. Between a mistyped digit and a flawed measurement methodology, asking how a number could fail catches both.
