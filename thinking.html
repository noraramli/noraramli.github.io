Thinking

One question, four lenses.

A list of principles costs nothing; the proof lies in what they do when set against a real problem. So here is one: a catastrophic flood, followed from the first drop of rain to the last bill, examined through the four lenses I reach for most.

The question

Who pays when the water rises?

December 2021: more than a month’s rain fell on the Klang Valley in a single day. Neighbourhoods with little memory of serious floods stood hip-deep in water, and losses across the country came to some RM6.1 billion.

Rainfall: Ministry of Environment and Water, December 2021. Losses: Department of Statistics Malaysia, January 2022.

Four mental models, each turned upon the same picture. Choose one, and see what it brings to light.

  1. The rain falls

    A December evening, and more rain than the drains were ever built for.

  2. Concrete, not green

    Where field and wetland once drank the rain, the tarmac passes it on to the next street.

  3. The home floods

    The water finds the lowest floor first and, too often, the family with no upstairs to climb to.

  4. A claim, if insured

    For some, a form and a settlement. For others, a mop and a long week.

  5. Next year’s premium

    The risk is now on the books, and the price follows it.

  6. Cover lapses

    Those with least to spare let it go first, just when they need it most.

  7. The public purse

    What insurance no longer carries, the state picks up, after the water has gone.

…and the next flood finds them poorer than the last. The loop closes.

Through this lens

Systems thinking

Pull one thread, and somewhere across the cloth a seam gives way.

Where does the water go next?

The flood begins upstream, long before the rain: in every field and wetland paved over. Nature is the first line of defence, yet sits on no one’s balance sheet.

Change a number, and little moves. Change what the business is for, and everything beneath it follows. I work where the leverage is – in the boardroom, where sustainability sets the tone rather than trailing behind it.

Through this lens

Antifragile

A glass shatters under strain; a bone thickens where the weight falls. One is fragile, the other antifragile.

When the worst arrives, what breaks, and what grows stronger?

A neighbourhood that has stayed dry for thirty years is not safe, it is merely untested. No evidence of a flood is not evidence of no flood. The illusion of comfort from a long, dry run shatters as the water rises; only what was built expecting it still stands strong.

Calm teaches a system nothing, until chaos comes along. I examine scenario after scenario, for the improbable is not the impossible, and the future no one rehearsed is the one that arrives.

Through this lens

Invert, invert, invert

Understand how to fail, then avoid it.

How would one make this worse?

Price the risk perfectly, and the poorest are the first to drop out. The risk stays behind, uninsured, until the next flood comes due. Precision, pursued alone, is its own kind of folly.

Before I trust a number, I ask how it could be wrong. Each answer becomes a control.

Through this lens

Iteration loops

Begin before it is perfect, and let each round feed the one after.

What does each flood teach the next?

Each flood is a lesson the system either learns or pays for again. Rebuild the house exactly as it stood, and the next flood finds it waiting. Rebuild it a little higher, a little wiser, and the loop begins to loosen.

No piece of work is ever final, only the best version so far. I put it to use early, listen to what it gets wrong, and let the next round put it right.

As one sees it

Nature’s wrath keeps no guest list, yet the most vulnerable are left with the most to mend. No one party can carry the burden alone: not the insurer, nor the state, nor the household.

Price the risk honestly, but look to Mother Nature.

Tend to her, and she will meet us halfway.
